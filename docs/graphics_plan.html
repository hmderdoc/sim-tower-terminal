xtrn/sim_tower · Go door · revised plan

SimTower on a terminal, three rows per floor

Every floor is three text rows tall so rooms carry real art and the tower moves: workers at desks, guests asleep under rising z's, elevator cars with doors that open, two-cell people walking the lobby. The mocks below are painted live by the same tile rules the door will use.

What changed from the first draft

  • Three rows per floor, one column per unit. A 9-unit office is a 9x3 scene. The original floor is 4.5 units tall at 8 px per unit; three 8x16 cells give 6 units, close enough that the tower keeps its proportions.
  • Responsive. 80x24 is the minimum: 7 floors by 76 units. The door probes the terminal and uses all of it. 132x50 shows 15 floors by 128 units, and SyncTERM at 80x60 shows 19 floors. Nothing is scaled, only the viewport.
  • Art per room, not patterns. Each item has a drawn tile with states (vacant, occupied, closed, lit, dirty, asleep, infested, burning). Signs carry the room's real name from the original list.
  • Animation. People walk (2 frames), elevator cars move in thirds of a floor and open their doors, escalators run, cinema screens flicker during shows, clouds drift, rain falls, fire flickers and spreads.
  • Zoom out. Z switches to a one-row-per-floor overview for navigating a 100-floor tower; it is a map, not the game view.

Tile sheet

Every item as drawn, with its states. Wall on the right edge of each room; colors from the 16-color PC palette so this works on SyncTERM, NetRunner, fTelnet and the Linux console. The tiles are data (rows of characters plus a paint map), so they live in a text asset the Go renderer loads and they can be edited without touching code.

Building, inspecting, elevators

The build palette (B or click BUILD) lists every item with a live preview of its tile, cost and star requirement; locked rows are grey. With a tool selected, moving the mouse over the tower shows the item's outline as a ghost (white) that turns red where placement is illegal with the reason on the message line. Left click places, the tool stays armed like the original; right click or Esc drops it. Elevator shafts drag vertically; lobby and floor drag horizontally; bulldoze drags across a run. Keyboard fallback: a cursor cell, arrows move, Enter places, Shift+arrows drag.

Inspecting a shaft opens the elevator panel: the shaft with its cars, served floors, and the original's per-period schedule (WD/WE, 7 periods) with Local / Express Up / Express Down per period, Waiting Car Response and Standard Floor Departure dials, and ADD CAR. Inspecting a room shows its facility window: name and floor, leasing state, evaluation bar, occupants, length of stay, rent or price dial, and the message line the original shows ("Office neighbor is too noisy").

Open choices

Floor height. Three rows is the default here. Two rows per floor is closer to the original's proportions (4 units tall vs 4.5) and shows 10 floors at 80x24, but people become one cell and beds and desks lose a row. Four rows gives richer art and 5 floors at 80x24. The tiles are data, so the renderer can carry more than one height if you want to compare in the terminal.

Truecolor. Spekder probes the terminal and uses 24-bit color where available. The tiles here are 16-color; a truecolor pass could soften walls, carpet and sky without changing the art. Deferred unless you want it in the first build.

Sprite people in the lobby. Two-cell people (head + body) with a two-frame walk. One-cell faces are kept for elevator cars and queues where space is tight.
